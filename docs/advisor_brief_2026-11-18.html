1. The reporting fix for Tables 1 and 3

Appendix B, Table 3 · same ε grid, same β

Table 3 reports tail welfare as 2.58362 ± 0.00643 against 2.58487 ± 0.00611. The difference is a fifth of either bar, so as printed the table supports no claim of separation. But the methods are run on the same instance, the same scenarios and the same draw — the comparison is paired, and the paired interval is the correct summary.

2. The tail result is real, not an in-sample artefact

New · §9.2 needs this to make its central claim

The obvious referee objection to any CVaR result is that CVaR fits the tail it was shown. So clear on one scenario set and evaluate the resulting day-ahead commitment on a disjoint set drawn from the same conditional pool.

This is the single most useful new number for the paper: 30/30 on held-out scenarios is what distinguishes "CVaR genuinely reduces tail loss" from "CVaR fits the in-sample tail", and the draft has never tested it.

3. A parameter-free way to quote the headline

Eq. (3) · the α+/α− deviation bid prices

The planner charges α± for deviating from the day-ahead schedule. In the code these were hard-coded as mc/10 and VOLL/10 with the comment "just a heuristic". They are not harmless: the value of risk-aware clearing is almost exactly linear in them, so any single headline $/MWh figure is a restatement of that constant.

Quote the slope, not the level: 12.225 $/MWh per unit δ, equivalently 1.2225% of the load's deviation bid price. That is a property of the uncertainty rather than of a tuned parameter, and it pre-empts the referee who assumes the result was bought with a large VOLL.

One theory gap found

Eq. (107) presumes ε < 1. At ε = 1 the tilted measure concentrates entirely on the tail, so ρ̃ω = 0 off it and pω = νω/ρ̃ω is a genuine 0/0 — real-time prices do not exist off the tail. The valid range is ε ∈ (0, 1), exactly where ρ̃ and ρ are mutually absolutely continuous. This is a companion to the existing ε > 0 hypothesis and needs one sentence in §8.5.1. Logged as T11.

The claim that did not reproduce

Needs a decision before submission

§9.2 states: "the value of risk-aware clearing becomes more pronounced as exposure to renewable uncertainty grows." Holding total installed capacity and load fixed and varying only the mix from 15% to 75% renewable, the value per MWh moves 1.2227 → 1.1698 $/MWh — a factor of 0.96, slightly down.

In the draft this claim rests on gaps of +0.0038 to +0.0102 against error bars of ±0.0064 to ±0.0246 — entirely inside the noise. So it was never supported, and now we have evidence pointing the other way.

The fix is to change the claim, and it becomes a stronger one. Renewable share is not the mechanism. Tail-heaviness of supply is. Separate experiments sweeping four independent physical drivers of a heavy supply tail — stress probability, stress depth, spatial correlation, and common-mode outage — show the advantage growing by 2.1× to 4.0×, with rank correlation 1.000 on all four. Those are the W-suite experiments; see below.

Who bears the tail risk

This supports Remarks 8 and 9 with measured numbers, which the draft does not currently have. Two honest caveats belong in the text:

  • πk is exactly zero for coal and nuclear. Thermal capacity in thermal_gens.csv is constant across scenarios, so an infra-marginal unit has constant loss L and Σω(q−ρ)L = L·0 = 0. Modelling forced outages would give thermal genuine tail exposure.
  • The Eq. (111) equal-share rule hides the signal. The flat pool share is −$104,273/h and swamps the per-agent term, so the net column is nearly identical across generators. A contribution-weighted δk would allocate it sensibly — that is a property of the rule, and worth a remark.

Robustness

The headline configuration (summer, 18:00) is the most favourable of the four. Quote the range $0.39–$1.22/MWh rather than the single best number; a referee will find this otherwise.

Still in flight

The tail-welfare suite (W0–W7) — the experiments carrying the paper's central claim about tail welfare — is running now as a separate 14-hour job. Until it lands, every tail-welfare number in the log comes from a local pilot at reduced scale, not from Gurobi at publishable scale. Those figures should not go in the paper yet.

One known limitation is worth stating because it bounds the scope of the claim rather than weakening it. Under the paper's formulation, generation capacity is exogenous and VOLL exceeds every marginal cost, so the planner always serves the physical maximum. Unserved energy is therefore identical under all three policies, measured to a relative 4.7×10−8. Risk-aware clearing reallocates adjustment cost; it cannot improve adequacy. Reporting that immediately after the tail results pre-empts the question rather than inviting it.
